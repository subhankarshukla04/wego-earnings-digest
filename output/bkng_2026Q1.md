## Booking Holdings (BKNG) · 2026Q1
*Booking flagged a near‑term MENA headwind from the Middle‑East conflict while accelerating AI‑driven personalization and cross‑sell initiatives.*

### What the firm said
Booking quantified the conflict’s drag on growth, estimating a 2‑percentage‑point hit to room‑night and gross‑booking growth and noting that, absent the impact, room nights would have risen about 8% (Glenn Fogel, CEO). The CFO added that MENA bookers accounted for roughly 4% of global room nights in 2025, rising to 7% when inbound travel is included (Ewout Steenbergen, CFO), and that a 3‑point headwind is baked into Q2 guidance. Marketing spend rose 16% YoY, with expense as a share of gross bookings up 4 bps to 3.8%, partly due to canceled paid‑channel bookings from the conflict (Steenbergen). Capital allocation remained aggressive, highlighted by a record $3.6 billion share‑repurchase program (Fogel). On the product side, Booking is embedding AI across the organization to boost efficiency and partner value, rolling out “Connected Trip” personalization, enhanced natural‑language search, and “smart filters” globally, while its Priceline “Penny” AI agent now offers conversational, multi‑product trip planning (Fogel). Cross‑sell momentum is strong, with connected transactions growing in the high‑teens and representing low double‑digit percentages of total bookings (Fogel). Finally, the firm is deepening API and NDC integrations with airlines and hotel chains to enrich the Connected Trip experience (Fogel).

### What this means for Wego
Booking’s conflict‑driven MENA headwind underscores the volatility of a region that already contributes a modest share of its global room‑night volume (≈4‑7%). Wego’s MENA‑first revenue mix (65% of revenue) should prioritize defensive brand and SEO investments to mitigate similar shocks, as paid‑channel volatility can erode margins. Booking’s AI‑driven personalization and conversational “Penny” layer have *no direct read for Wego on AI‑driven conversational booking*—Wego remains a referrals‑only platform without an AI front‑end, so the competitive lever is brand trust and organic‑search depth rather than feature parity. The push to integrate airlines and hotels via APIs and NDC highlights a supply‑side trend; Wego can leverage its existing airline partners (Emirates, 5 Pakistani carriers, Malaysia Airlines) and hotel partners (IHG, Accor) to ensure inventory richness, but must accept that deeper API integration is not currently in its roadmap. Booking’s cross‑sell growth suggests that bundling multiple verticals boosts conversion; Wego should explore modest cross‑linking of flight‑hotel combos within its referral pages to capture incremental referrals without building a checkout funnel.
